Explore the Heart of Naxos: A Route of Cultural Interest

Far from the crowded beaches, beats the heart of what we locals call the “real” Naxos. There is far more to this heavenly island than what can be seen at a first glimpse.

Embark on an unforgettable journey through the lush, history-rich landscapes of central Naxos with the Route of Cultural Interest — a scenic and diverse trail that weaves through ancient heritage sites, traditional villages, and verdant valleys fed by natural springs.

Whether you're a history enthusiast, nature lover, or cultural explorer, the Route of Cultural Interest offers a deeply enriching experience through the authentic heart of Naxos. Lace up your walking shoes and step into a world where ancient myths, medieval towers, and timeless traditions meet under the Aegean sun.

Begin Your Journey in Agii Apostoli, Melanes
Your adventure begins at Agii Apostoli, the largest settlement in the village of Melanes, offering two equally captivating paths:
  1. Route One leads to the hilltop village of Kourounochori, where you’ll discover the Sommaripa/Fragopoulos Tower House, one of Naxos’s oldest and most impressive fortified mansions, set against a backdrop of terraced hills.
  2. Route Two winds through the lush Melanes Valley, past the Church of Agios Georgios, a former Roman structure that evolved into one of the island’s earliest Christian places of worship, adorned with frescoes from multiple historical eras.

Historic Landmarks & Natural Beauty

As the two routes converge on a narrow stone walkway to Myli, you’ll encounter centuries-old watermills nestled in an idyllic setting. The path continues along the ancient elliptical aqueduct, guiding you toward Flerio, home to:
  • The Sanctuary of the Springs
  • The half-finished 6th-century BC Kouroi statues carved into the rock at the ancient quarries. 

A Living Tapestry of Rural Life

From Flerio, follow a wide, gently rising trail toward Potamia, passing well-preserved threshing floors and lime furnaces along the way. A stunning marble-paved footpath leads to Ano Potamia, famous for its ancient water fountain and timeless architectural character.

Explore the three charming Potamia villages — Ano (Upper), Messi (Middle), and Kato (Lower) — where time seems to stand still amid traditional chapels, tower houses, olive presses, and stone watermills.
  • At Platanaki, relax by the shaded stream and admire the stone-built laundry station
  • Take a brief detour at Lierado to see the 17th-century Kokkos Tower, overlooking the gully
  • Pass through fragrant fruit orchards en route to Episkopi, where the majestic Byzantine Church of Agios Mamas (Theoskepasti) awaits, showcasing rare frescoes and inscriptions.
